A. GAME OVERVIEW, PLAYERS, COMPONENTS, AND OBJECTIVE
- Game title: Modern Mint.
- Standard game: 2–6 players, approximately 60–150 minutes, age 14+.
- Standard game lasts 9 rounds.
- The game has Founders and Investors. For 4–6 players, choose 2 Investors and make all remaining players Founders. For 2–3 players, all human players are Founders and one Dummy Investor is selected at random.
- At the end of the standard game, winners are determined by Net Value:
  Net Value = sum of (equity percentage owned in each company × that company’s valuation at its current stage) + cash in hand − outstanding loans including unpaid interest.
- In a 2–3-player game, there is one winner: the Founder with the highest Net Value.
- In a 4–6-player game, there are two category winners: the Founder with the highest Founder Net Value and the Investor with the highest Investor Net Value.
- Track each player's cash, equity/ownership, investments, royalty/revenue-sharing rights, loans, interest, company stages, team levels, action points, and relevant card effects accurately.
- Physical-board components described by the rulebook include a main board, 12 Event Cards, 36 Mentor Cards, 4 Founder Player Mats, 2 Investor Player Mats, a D10 die, a Round Tracker coin, 4 Turn Order tokens (one per Founder), 1 Bankruptcy Action Card, 40 Founder Sheets, 20 Investor Sheets, 6 Dummy Investor Cards, 4 Action Point Tracker discs, 24 Team Tokens (12 for Team 2, 8 for Team 3, 4 for Team 4), four stage cubes per Founder marked L/R/G/S, six markers with erasers, currency notes, and the rulebook. The digital game should implement the corresponding game systems and all available card effects/data.

- In a 4–6-player game, all Founder pitches should be heard before Investors finalize allocations. Investors negotiate terms and then commit funds.

- Investor role: observe Founder decisions, optionally provide advice, and record strategy/portfolio information. Investors do not take Founder company actions unless the existing game rules explicitly allow it.

- After each Founder completes Phase II, advance that Founder’s Action Point marker by the number of actions taken.

D. PHASE I — FUNDRAISING AND BANK LOANS
1. Fundraising does not cost Action Points.
2. Founders can raise money from Investors and/or other Founders. All deals must be mutually agreed; record investor/funder, recipient, amount, company, deal type, ownership/royalty/interest terms, duration/trigger, and repayment terms.
3. In 4–6 player games, Investors hear all Founder proposals before making/finalizing allocations. Investors can invest in one or more Founders/companies and negotiate valuation and terms.
4. The rulebook lists the following deal types:
   a. Equity Deal: Investor provides money in exchange for a percentage of company ownership. Return is based on that equity percentage multiplied by the company’s end-game stage valuation.
   b. Royalty/Revenue-Sharing Deal: Investor provides money for an agreed percentage of company revenue each round. No equity is granted. Payments continue for the agreed duration or until an agreed total repayment/return cap is reached.
   c. Loan Deal: Investor lends money at an agreed interest rate per round. No equity or revenue share is granted. Principal and accumulated interest are repaid according to the agreed terms.
   d. Hybrid Deal: Combine two permitted deal types, e.g., equity + loan or equity + royalty. Track and settle each component separately.
   e. Convertible Note/Innovative Deal: A loan can convert into equity under clearly specified conditions (e.g., trigger round/stage and valuation cap). Before conversion, it behaves as a loan; after conversion, it behaves as equity. If conversion does not occur, use the agreed repayment/interest terms.
